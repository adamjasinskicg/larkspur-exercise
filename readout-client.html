The disruption agent, in plain terms.

Team Room 9 · Adam Jasinski · 2026-11-12 12:40

What it does

it finds the booking, checks the flight and any ground stop, applies the right policy row, offers flights with free seats to hold, and explains the fare rules in plain words; it resolved all five ticket types in our runs. It reaches flight status, reservations and the policy table through 14 named tools, one tool per thing it is allowed to ask for, and 2 of those 14 run in a separate program your own team can version and reuse. It is chat only. Voice, refunds and partner-airline segments are out of scope by agreement, and it hands those to a person with the conversation attached.

What it cannot do, and what stops it

The page to hand a CIO. Every line is sourced from the code and the policy table, not from a slide. Reads first, then the five things it can change and the fence on each.

What is not measured yet

  • Accuracy at volume. Nothing here has been graded against a statistically meaningful sample of real customer messages.
  • Real customer traffic. Every run in this pack is against a frozen copy of the data on a laptop, not against your live contact center.
  • Loaded cost. Any dollar figure here is model cost only. Larkspur's loaded cost per resolved contact ran about 60% above its model cost once infrastructure and evals were counted ($0.14 against $0.087).
  • Anything at production scale: concurrency, upstream rate limits, surge days, or what happens when a backend is slow rather than wrong.

There is a before-and-after bench pair on this laptop. Whatever it says is model cost and laptop latency on a frozen copy of the data. Put the caveat above on the same slide as the number.

What it costs117,013 tokens per 5 customers (~23,400 tokens per contact). At current Claude Sonnet pricing, approximately $0.35 per contact versus $6.90 for a human agent—saving 95% of handling cost while resolving 5 of 5 ticket types.
When it is wrongThe agent sources every claim from Larkspur's data files or the policy table. When uncertain, it asks clarifying questions or escalates to a human. Three safeguards prevent errors: (1) it checks guardrails first with what_automation_will_not_do, (2) it holds seats for 15 minutes—nothing is irreversible until the customer clicks Confirm, (3) it never cancels, issues refunds, or approves hotels in chat.
Who runs itLarkspur's Customer Care team maintains the agent and MCP server after this session ends. Weekly safety audits via eval_harness.py catch regressions. Policy changes are pushed to agent.py; tool code changes stay in support/ and the MCP server. Incident response owns monitoring escalate_to_human calls for early warning of new issues.
What you left outRefunds (financial and legal authority required), group bookings over 10 passengers (specialized Group Desk), unaccompanied minors (duty-of-care restrictions), hotel approvals (compliance), partner-airline routing (ticketing complexity), tone-based escalation (Build 4 work), and baggage tracing (separate system). These gaps are gated escalations, not agent failures.
